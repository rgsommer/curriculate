Who We Are

A school in the service of families, the church, and the formation of persons.

Oak Hill Academy opened its doors in September 2018 in Hamilton, Ontario. We are a community of families whose imaginations have been captured by the rich classical Christian tradition — and by the children in our care.

Our Story

From Moses and Homer to Dante and Lewis.

From Moses and Homer to Dante, John Calvin, and Teresa of Ávila, down through to Thomas Merton, Dorothy Sayers, and C.S. Lewis, the end of such an education is not merely a career, but the cultivation of the human spirit: "teaching the young to know what is good, to serve it above self, to reproduce it, and to recognize that in knowledge lies responsibility" (David V. Hicks, Norms and Nobility).

We desire to see our children strive to be sages and saints, to love God and neighbour, to know their heritage and home, and to carry their child-like wonder with them all their lives.

Children Are Born Persons

The landscape is rich. The plot is thick. The stakes are high.

As a community of families, our hearts have been captured by our children. Through their eyes we see again that the world is enchanted, just as it is. Through caring for them we inch closer to knowing what it means to be loved by God. And through them we are reminded of to whom belongs the kingdom of heaven.

The landscape is rich: fauns and frogs, knights and knaves, sorcerers and scientists, Pharaohs and philosophers, and the underestimated carpenter, peasant, and hobbit. The plot is thick: war and peace, pride and prejudice, irony, accident, and divine comedy. The stakes are high: heaven and hell weigh in the balance. And here come the persons we call children — skipping, stumbling, singing, stopping to watch a snail, finding their place in the Story.

Radix coronam nutrit. — The root nourishes the crown. Welcome to Oak Hill.

Our Distinctives

Six commitments that shape every classroom and conversation.

Christian

We delight in the Triune God. We delight in the Scriptures, the Gospel of grace, the Church, and the Great Commission. We stand in the historic orthodox Christian faith — the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds — and in the Reformed tradition.

Classical

The heartbeat of the classical tradition is forming virtue and flourishing culture through participation in Truth, Goodness, and Beauty. Our students are masters of language and math, able to think, write, and speak with clarity, confidence, and charm.

Restful

The English word "school" comes from the Greek scholé — leisure, or unhurried time to think about the things most worthwhile. Multum non multa: much, not many. Festina lente: make haste slowly. Mastery, not finishing a textbook.

Embodied

Our first home was not a climate-controlled box, but a garden — and the first human a gardener. God made us as body-soul unions. We honour the body God gave us in song, recitation, handwriting, handicraft, and time outdoors.

Integrated

History is the spine: an unfolding story cycled through Ancient, Medieval, Renaissance, and Modern periods. Living books, narration, real dialogue, and living ideas tie literature, math, science, art, and worship into one coherent whole.

Communal

Oak Hill serves as an extension of the family and the church. God has given parents the privileged responsibility of raising their children in the instruction and paideia of the Lord. We partner with parents in the wholesome growth of their children.

Educational Priorities

Five things we will not compromise.

We believe children are embodied image-bearers of God, made to behold God's glory — to which all the world is the theatre and redemption the story. From that conviction flow these five priorities.

Tune the heart to love goodness, truth, and beauty.

Education is about formation, not information. Before we ask what a student knows, we ask what a student loves.

Furnish the imagination with living books and great art.

An integrated curriculum of literature, music, painting, and the great stories of human history — the cultural inheritance from which the western world continually sprang.

Train the mind to master language and mathematical arts.

The seven liberal arts — Trivium (grammar, dialectic, rhetoric) and Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music) — taught with both rigour and joy.

Expand capacities for attention, appreciation, and craft.

"Imitation precedes art." We slow down, look long, and learn from the masters before we ask students to make work of their own.

Work with our hands and enjoy nature.

An atmosphere less institutional and more like home, garden, studio, and cathedral.
