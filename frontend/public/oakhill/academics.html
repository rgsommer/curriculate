Academics

The classical tradition, faithfully taught.

An integrated curriculum built around the seven liberal arts, history as its spine, and the great books, art, and ideas of the western world.

What is Classical Education?

An education older than the printing press.

Classical education is the education that formed the West for two thousand years — the one Augustine, Aquinas, Calvin, Bach, Pascal, Newton, and Lewis all received. It rests on three convictions: that truth is real and knowable, that beauty draws the soul, and that virtue can be cultivated.

Practically, it means an integrated curriculum of grammar, logic, rhetoric, arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and music — taught through history's unfolding story and through the living books that shaped it.

See the three stages
The Trivium

Three stages, twelve years, one shaped soul.

The Trivium meets students where they are. Grammar in the early years, when memory is strongest. Logic in middle school, when questioning begins. Rhetoric in the high school years, when the voice matures.

i.

Grammar Stage

JK – Grade 4

The poll-parrot years. Children memorize joyfully — Scripture, poetry, Latin chants, math facts, geography, and the names of God's creatures. We fill the storehouse, knowing later years will draw on it.

ii.

Logic Stage

Grades 5 – 8

The pert and questioning years. Students learn formal logic, follow arguments, spot fallacies, and write essays of real substance. We welcome — and discipline — their growing sense of "but why?"

iii.

Rhetoric Stage

Grades 9 – 12

The poetic years. Students learn to speak and write with clarity, confidence, and charm. They apply their formed minds to vocation, the great conversation, and the questions that matter most.

An Integrated Curriculum

History is the organizing spine.

Rather than a string of disconnected subjects, our curriculum is built around the great unfolding story of human history — Ancient, Medieval, Renaissance, and Modern. Each cycle takes four years; students walk through the cycle three times across grades 1–12, each time at greater depth.

Literature, science, art, and worship come along — students read The Iliad while studying ancient Greece, build a model trebuchet while studying the medieval world, and read Bonhoeffer while learning the twentieth century. Knowledge is woven, not stacked.

The Seven Liberal Arts

The arts of a free person.

"Liberal" comes from libertas: free. These are the arts a free citizen needs to think, judge, and act well in the world.

The Trivium

Grammar

The art of language well-ordered. We learn the structure of English and the elegance of Latin from the early grades.

The Trivium

Dialectic

The art of reasoning rightly. Formal logic in the middle grades teaches students how to follow and form an argument.

The Trivium

Rhetoric

The art of persuasive, beautiful speech. Students learn to write essays, deliver speeches, and engage in real dialogue.

The Quadrivium

Arithmetic

Number in itself. From singing skip-counts in JK to algebra and beyond, math is taught as a discipline of beauty as well as utility.

The Quadrivium

Geometry

Number in space. Euclidean geometry trains the eye and the proof-making mind together.

The Quadrivium

Astronomy

Number in time. Wonder at the heavens leads naturally to physics, calendar, and the praise of the Maker.

The Quadrivium

Music

Number in motion. Children sing daily; older students take up instruments, harmony, and theory — the most embodied of the mathematical arts.

Living Books, Living Ideas

We read the best, and we read it carefully.

A "living book" — Charlotte Mason's phrase — is a book written by an author in love with the subject, capable of communicating that love to a child. We use a few great books deeply rather than many textbooks shallowly.

Daily practices include narration (students retell what they have read or heard, in their own words), copywork (the careful imitation of beautiful sentences), recitation, nature study, and the slow, attentive viewing of great works of art.

  • Living books over textbooks
  • Narration over multiple-choice
  • Mastery over coverage
  • Beauty over busyness
"Children are born persons." — Charlotte Mason, 20 Principles
"The goal is mastery, not finishing a textbook." Multum non multa — festina lente. — Christopher Perrin
Grades Offered

Junior Kindergarten through Grade Twelve.

Oak Hill is adding a grade or two each year as our students grow. By the 2026–27 school year we will offer the full JK–12 classical Christian sequence.

Kindergarten

JK & SK

Three full days per week, in two cohorts: Monday/Wednesday/Thursday or Tuesday/Wednesday/Friday. JK students must be 4 by December 31.

Grammar

Grades 1 – 4

Five days a week. Daily Scripture, math, phonics, literature, recitation, Latin, music, art, and nature study.

Logic

Grades 5 – 8

Five days a week. Formal logic enters; literature and history deepen; Latin continues; math reaches pre-algebra and algebra.

Rhetoric

Grades 9 – 12

High school in operation by 2026–27. A senior thesis, the great books, advanced math and sciences, and rhetoric in action.
