Every May, in playgrounds and on tracks, the same scene plays out. A PE teacher with a stopwatch in one hand and a soggy clipboard in the other. A volunteer parent squinting at a sheet trying to remember which kid was in the second heat. A laminated points table on a folding table in the wind. And, after it's all over, somebody's evening on a kitchen-table spreadsheet trying to decide which kid actually won the Age 8 Girls overall.

We've watched it. We've been the one with the clipboard. We built Field Day so it doesn't have to keep happening.

Today, Field Day is live — at www.curriculate.net/fieldday — and it's free for every school.

What it is

Field Day is a single web app with three jobs:

  • Help event leaders run their event — pick a title from a dropdown, set age and gender, add competitors, and capture results. Timed events get a built-in stopwatch accurate to the hundredth of a second; field events handle distance or weight with best-of-N attempts.
  • Score the day automatically — 5-4-3-2-1 points roll up by age and gender as soon as leaders submit. Ties handle themselves, your way: average tied positions, or both get the higher place. Toggle in Settings; everything recalculates instantly.
  • Make the announcer's life easy — the moment a leader hits Submit, the completed category lines up in the Announcer tab with title, age, gender, and the medal-by-medal results. Tap "Announced ✓" and the next one slides in.

And when the day is done, the Ribbons tab gives you a single, print-ready sheet of every place in every event, sorted exactly the way you'll write the ribbons.

The whole thing replaces a folder full of paper sheets, three different spreadsheets, and the announcer asking "Wait, who came third?" — with one app that everybody on the field can use.

Built for the people who actually run the day

The single biggest design decision was deciding who the user is. Field Day is built for the volunteer parent at Station 4. The PE teacher juggling six events. The grandparent at the mic stand. None of them want to set up an account, install something from an app store, or learn a new tool the morning of. So they don't have to.

For the Admin

The admin signs in once with their email and gets a passkey on screen. They name their school and pick a school code. That's it — they're in. They see every event in the school, every points total, every competitor.

For Event Leaders

Leaders join with the school code and their name. No email, no password, no account. Ten seconds, and they're at a clean Events screen ready to spin up their first race.

For the Announcer

The announcer just opens the Announce tab. Big titles, names with results, one button. The queue stays full as leaders submit; they never have to chase anybody for a printout.

How scoring works

The points table is the standard one most schools use:

  • 1st place: 5 points
  • 2nd place: 4 points
  • 3rd place: 3 points
  • 4th place: 2 points
  • Participation: 1 point

Ties get the trickiest part of any field day right. We support two methods, and the admin chooses which one:

Average tied positions. Two kids tie for 1st: each gets (5+4)/2 = 4.5 points. The next kid gets 3rd. Mathematically fair, and what most middle and high schools use.

Both get the higher placement. Two kids tie for 1st: each gets 5 points. The next kid gets 3rd. Easier to explain, more encouraging — what most elementary schools do.

Switch any time. Rankings recalculate instantly across every event in the school.

Privacy and data, plainly

Field Day stores its data in your browser. There is no central database holding student names. The app works without internet, by design — so a Wi-Fi outage on the day doesn't take down the field. When you want to sync results across devices (admin tablet plus a few leader tablets, say), use Export Data and Import Data. Move a JSON file via AirDrop, email, or a USB stick.

That's a deliberate trade-off. We give up a little real-time-multi-device convenience to give you back full control over student data and a tool that simply works on the day, regardless of what your school's network is doing.
