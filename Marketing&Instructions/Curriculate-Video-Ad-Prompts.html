Diagonal Split-Screen Campaign

Series Overview

Concept
Each video uses a diagonal split screen. The left side shows a cartoon teacher buried in grading. The right side shows the same character living their best life. The contrast is instant and emotional.
Format
9:16 vertical (1080×1920). Diagonal split runs top-left to bottom-right. Left panel is desaturated/stressed. Right panel is vibrant/joyful.
Duration
8–10 seconds each. First 3s shows left panel (drudgery), next 3s reveals right panel (freedom), final 2–4s is full-screen CTA with logo.
Style
2D cartoon illustration, Pixar-adjacent character design. Consistent character within each video (varies between videos). Clean lines, expressive faces.
AI Tool
Runway Gen-3 Alpha Turbo (or Gen-4 if available). Generate left and right panels as separate clips, composite in CapCut or Premiere with diagonal mask.
Voiceover
Generate with ElevenLabs or Runway's built-in voice. Warm, relatable, slightly humorous tone. Alternate male/female voices across the series.
Music
Each video has a deliberate audio shift from stressed/muted (left) to upbeat/warm (right). Use royalty-free or AI-generated music. The transition should be sharp and satisfying.
Distribution
Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels. Can also be used as paid ads on Meta and TikTok.

Production Notes
Post-Production Workflow
Compositing the Diagonal Split
Generate left and right panels as separate 9:16 clips in Runway. In CapCut or Premiere Pro, place both on the timeline and apply a diagonal mask (top-left to bottom-right, roughly 45 degrees). Add a thin white or gradient line along the diagonal for visual separation. The left clip plays first (0–3s), then the right clip wipes in along the diagonal (3–6s), then both dissolve to a full-screen branded end card (6–8s).
End Card Template
Curriculate gradient background (blue → purple). Large white logo centered. Tagline below in white. "curriculate.net" at the bottom. Keep it clean — 2 seconds is enough. Consider adding a subtle shimmer or particle animation.
Character Consistency Tip
Within each video, the left and right character must feel like the same person. Use Runway's "image to video" feature: generate a still character design first, then use that as the reference image for both left and right clips. This keeps proportions, hair, and face consistent across the split.
Voiceover Generation
Use ElevenLabs with a warm, mid-30s voice. For videos 1, 3, 5: female voice. For videos 2, 4, 6: male voice. Keep the delivery conversational, not announcer-y. The "this... or this" rhythm should feel like a friend nudging you, not a sales pitch.
